Pictures that hold their breath

Watercolour and ink, mostly wet-on-wet, mostly about water.

I draw for picture books, magazines and the occasional label. Every piece starts on 300gsm cotton paper and ends as a layered file — so the wash keeps its bloom and the art director keeps their deadline.

How a piece gets made

Four washes

01

Thumbnails

Twenty tiny drawings the size of a stamp, in pencil, before anything is precious. We pick two. — one day

02

Underdrawing

Tight graphite on cotton rag, taped down and stretched. This is the stage for changing your mind about the composition. — two days

03

Wet-on-wet

The paper is soaked and the colour is dropped in. Nothing here can be undone, which is the whole point of it. — the good part

04

Ink & hand-off

Line work over the dry wash, then scanned at 1200dpi and delivered as layered TIFF plus flattened CMYK. — one day

The studio

A desk, a jar, and salt

I work from a second-floor room in Valparaíso with one window that fogs up whenever the kettle goes on. Most mornings start with a wash test on scrap paper to see what the humidity is doing — it decides how the day will go more than any plan does.

Before illustration I spent six years as a marine science technician, which is where the whales came from. I've since made pictures for four picture books, two of which you can find in libraries, and a great many magazine spreads about the sea.

  • BasedValparaíso, Chile — working GMT-4
  • ToolsWinsor & Newton pans, Arches 300gsm, Pentel pocket brush
  • DeliversLayered TIFF, flattened CMYK, print-ready at 1200dpi
  • TeachingWet-on-wet workshop, twice yearly
