About us

We opened with a soldering iron, not a shop.

Fourteen years later the repair bench is still upstairs — which is the whole reason we're careful about what goes on the shelf downstairs.

In 2011, Sanjay Patil rented a two-metre counter on Rajarampuri Main Road and replaced cracked screens for ₹400 a time.

Customers kept asking the same question while they waited: which one should I buy next? He'd seen the insides of every model on the market, so he had opinions — usually the unfashionable ones. Phones that were easy to open. Batteries that hadn't swollen. Charging ports that survived a monsoon.

That's how the shelf started. It has never been about carrying everything. If we can't get spare parts for a phone within a week, we don't sell it, however good the launch event was.

Today there are six of us. Sanjay still opens at ten, and still fixes the difficult ones himself.

How a phone gets on our shelf

Four things, in order

  1. Someone here carries it for a week

    Real SIM, real apps, real Kolhapur summer. If the phone gets uncomfortably hot on a bike, we note it.

  2. We open one up

    How hard is a battery swap? Is the charging port soldered to the board? This decides what a repair will cost you in year three.

  3. We check parts supply

    We call our Pune supplier. If a screen can't reach us in seven days, the phone doesn't come in.

  4. We write down who it's for

    Every model on the shelf has one sentence on its tag saying who should buy it — and who shouldn't.

What we won't do

Sell you insurance you'll never claim

We don't push extended warranties. The bench upstairs is cheaper than most of them, and you can watch the work.

What we insist on

Showing you the phone switched on

Every box is opened at the counter, the phone is charged, the screen is checked, and you leave with it working.

What we're bad at

Having the newest launch on day one

We usually stock a phone six weeks late, once we know how it holds up. If you want it on launch day, we'll say so honestly.
